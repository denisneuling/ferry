# Mock NAS

A stand-in for a household NAS, for developing ferry's interface and for
demonstrations: Samba 4.15 (Ubuntu 22.04) with the settings NAS
distributions ship, two people, their shares and a few hundred generated
sample files. Everything in it is fictional and generated by `gen/`; nothing
comes from a real NAS.

    make dev-nas          # build (the first time takes a minute) and start on 127.0.0.1:4455
    make run-nas          # ferry against it on http://localhost:8095
    make dev-nas-check    # that it is what this page says
    make dev-nas-stop     # stop it; it starts fresh next time

`CONTAINER=docker` works as everywhere in the Makefile.

## People

| user | password | in group |
|---|---|---|
| `alex` | `alex-nas-demo` | `family` |
| `sam` | `sam-nas-demo` | `family` |

Demo values, valid only in this container.

## Shares

| share | who | what |
|---|---|---|
| `alex` | alex only | photos (two albums, a panorama, a screenshot), documents with deep folders, 250 letters in one folder, a photo archive of 60000 files in one folder, a voice note, a 1.5 GB video |
| `sam` | sam only | concert photos, a thesis PDF, notes |
| `Family` | both, read and write; `force group = family`, `create mask = 0664` | holiday and birthday photos, recipes, insurance PDFs, a CSV |
| `Public` | both, read only | manuals (PDF), two tones (WAV), a wallpaper |
| `Scans` | both, read and write | fifteen scanned PDFs, named by date |

alex and sam cannot open each other's personal share. Whether they see it in
the list depends on `access based share enum`: off by default, as most NAS
ship it, on with `make dev-nas DEV_NAS_ABSE=yes`.

## The files

- **Photos:** JPEGs at camera sizes (12 to 24 megapixels, ~3 MB each), with
  EXIF orientations 1, 3, 6 and 8, stored turned as phones store them, so a
  viewer that ignores the tag shows them sideways. Their EXIF names
  fictional cameras (Fictional Optics, Harbour Camera Co.) with a time, an
  exposure and a lens; every third photo has a GPS position out on the
  Baltic Sea, where nobody lives.
- **Documents:** valid multi-page PDFs with a title in their info
  dictionary, laid out like a fictional firm's letter (a coloured
  letterhead, text lines, a table with a total) so that their thumbnails
  read as documents; the ones in `Scans` lie a little askew on a grey
  scanner bed. Markdown, plain text, a CSV.
- **Audio:** WAV files that play.
- **Video:** `Urlaub am Meer 🌊 2025.mp4` is 1.5 GB of zeros
  (`MOCK_NAS_VIDEO_SIZE`) with a video's name: enough to stream, seek and
  cancel, not to watch. It is made sparse when the container starts.
- **A large folder:** `alex/Fotoarchiv` holds 60000 empty files named
  `IMG_00001.jpg` and on (`MOCK_NAS_ARCHIVE_SIZE`), made when the container
  starts: a listing far beyond one batch, and more tiles than a browser can
  render at once.
- **Names** with umlauts, spaces, `&` and emoji, folders five levels deep,
  an empty folder, modification times spread over two years.

## Previous versions

`Family` keeps snapshots as a ZFS NAS with sanoid does, offered through
Samba's `vfs_shadow_copy2` (`snapshots.sh`, `smb.conf`): two of the share
(`autosnap_2026-11-21_03:00:00_daily`, `autosnap_2026-11-22_15:00:09_hourly`)
and one of `Fotos`, a nested dataset with its own
(`autosnap_2026-11-01_00:00:01_monthly`). In them
`Haushalt/Einkaufsliste.txt` has two earlier states, `Rezepte/Linsensuppe.md`
and `Fotos/Geburtstag Oma/Foto 1 (verwackelt).jpg` were deleted since, and
`Urlaubsplanung 2027.md` is new since. The snapshot folders are hidden from
listings, as ZFS hides them.

## Samba settings

`smb.conf` holds what ferry should expect from a real NAS: `map to guest =
never`, `server min protocol = LANMAN2`, `server max protocol = SMB3_11`,
`nt pipe support = yes`, `force group` and masks on the shared shares. The
tests' Samba (`hack/samba`) covers the other side — `map to guest = Bad
User` and `Bad Password` — and newer versions.
